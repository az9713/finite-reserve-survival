# Verification design for paid autonomous organization

The observable is the probability of remaining functional throughout a declared time interval. All arms start dispersed with the same initial chemical inventory, assembly materials, functional state, and spatial law. A terminal observation on an uncensored chain measures functional occupancy and can count paths that already failed and recovered.

## Physical and comparator checks

- Write a closed, uncensored state graph with a transition ledger. Every physical forward channel has an available reverse when the destination can supply its reactants. Check conservation of material, the initial preparation, the energy change per channel, and its rate ratio including combinatorial multiplicities. In dimensionless units the energy part of the ratio is \(\exp(-\Delta E)\). Changes to kinetic barriers affect both directions of the same physical edge. If opposite directions use different local chemical states for allosteric control, check the reverse at the actual destination and account for any energy difference.
- Passive binding or an attractive equilibrium potential can concentrate mass while paying a one-time preparation cost. Let the fixed family access the same passive mechanism and launch dispersion. If potentials differ between internal modes, perform mode changes at the current separation with the resulting energy difference in the ledger. If mode changes create a positional jump, specify its transition law and reverse. A localization penalty or unrestricted positional reset can predetermine the result.
- Use the same capacity, diffusion, reaction geometry and available resource budget in both arms. The fixed class should contain all parameter combinations admitted by the stated fixed architecture, including passive binding and disabled organization where those choices are physically available. State-dependent binding or release must be generated by local kinetics without observation-dependent interventions. A distinct catalyst or allosteric scaffold is part of the design and its availability must be stated. An advantage over a fixed architecture does not imply superiority over all passive architectures with other couplings.
- Replay signed event counts. With \(r\) repair fuel uses and \(o\) organizational fuel uses, both signed, a shared pool has \(b_t=b_0-r-o\) and \(w_t=w_0+r+o\). Record positive and negative event counts separately as a check on fuel recovery. Closed equilibrium cycles have zero total log rate ratio and zero net energy change. First failure retains the pre-failure killing rate from the full generator.

## Global fixed-design bound

Let \(Q(\theta)\) be the killed finite generator and \(S(\theta)=\mu e^{TQ(\theta)}\mathbf 1\), with a common, normalized launch law \(\mu\). Use the infinity matrix norm. A sub-Markov semigroup is a contraction in this norm. Duhamel's formula integrated over \([0,T]\) therefore gives \(|\partial_j S|\le T\|\partial_jQ\|_\infty\). Differentiating again gives the bound

\[
|\partial_{jk} S|\le T\|\partial_{jk}Q\|_\infty + T^2\|\partial_jQ\|_\infty\|\partial_kQ\|_\infty.
\]

The second term combines the two possible time orderings of one insertion of each first derivative. Each ordered integration region has area \(T^2/2\). Consequently the upper bound on a box centered at \(c\) with half-widths \(r_j\) is the upper endpoint of \(S(c)\), plus \(\sum_j r_j|\partial_jS(c)|\), plus half the sum of \(r_jr_k\) times the displayed derivative bounds taken over the box. Cover the entire parameter domain, include its boundaries, and subdivide boxes whose upper bound could improve the sampled lower bound. A stationary point or a dense scan alone supplies no upper bound.

The implemented fixed family uses \(Q(\alpha)=A+\alpha B\) for \(0\le\alpha\le1\). Here \(A\) comprises diffusion, damage, repair and passive tethering, while \(B\) is the conservative paid-tether channel at full conductance. Let \(q_0,\omega\) dominate the exits of \(A,B\), and define \(P=I+A/q_0\) and \(R=I+B/\omega\). The finite chain is the interleaving of independent Poisson proposal streams with rates \(q_0\) and \(\alpha\omega\). Conditional on \(n\) base proposals and \(m\) paid proposals, their order is uniform. Counting the possibilities for the first proposal gives

\[
v_{n,m}=\frac{n}{n+m}P v_{n-1,m}+\frac{m}{n+m}R v_{n,m-1},\qquad v_{0,0}=\mathbf 1,
\]

where a term with zero scalar coefficient is omitted. With \(\lambda=\omega T\), \(u_m=\sum_n\Pr(\operatorname{Pois}(q_0T)=n)\mu v_{n,m}\), and \(M\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\alpha\lambda)\),

\[
S(\alpha)=\mathbb E[u_M],\quad S'(\alpha)=\lambda\mathbb E[u_{M+1}-u_M],\quad
S''(\alpha)=\lambda^2\mathbb E[u_{M+2}-2u_{M+1}+u_M].
\]

Every exact \(v_{n,m}\) and \(u_m\) belongs to \([0,1]\). This gives paid-tail bounds of \(1,1,2\) before the corresponding factors of \(\lambda\). The maximum omitted paid-tail mass over the covered interval is attained at \(\alpha=1\) by Poisson stochastic ordering. Bound \(|S''|\) by the largest resolved second-difference magnitude plus twice the maximum remaining Poisson tail, all multiplied by \(\lambda^2\). Use this bound and the interval for \(S'(c)\) in the one-dimensional Taylor box bound. Splitting the box with the largest upper bound preserves the cover of \([0,1]\). The largest retained upper bound and best point lower bound enclose the supremum.

The independent solver represents declared decimal constants and squared lattice separations as exact rationals. It encloses rate exponentials, kernel entries and Poisson probabilities using directed Decimal arithmetic. A nonnegative kernel product is monotone in its entries. Conservative padding rounds each positive binary64 matrix-vector sum and weighted sum outwards, using the row's term-count bound and an underflow allowance. Separate geometric Poisson tails bound the truncations of both proposal streams. The spectator value uses independent single-kernel uniformization with the same rate-enclosure and rounding procedure. Dense matrix exponentials and direct jump paths exercise the channel enumeration and are additional checks on different steps of the calculation.

## Independent checks and scope

- Assemble a small unreduced periodic generator and compare its killed survival using a dense exponential. Include wrapped contact edges, mode switches, all reverse channels, an initial functional launch, and the distinction between first failure and steady occupancy. Use exact finite-state balance, reversibility, killing and resource identities as separate tests.
- Independently sample exponential event times and channel marks on the finite spatial graph. Stop paths at first failure, verify the material and signed fuel ledger, and report familywise simultaneous survival error using the Dvoretzky–Kiefer–Wolfowitz bound. Use a disjoint predetermined seed for each arm. A finite-grid path check tests the implemented Markov chain; off-grid Brownian accuracy requires a further method and a separate error statement.
- At the declared common horizons and kinetic envelope, compare a succession of spatial discretizations or report explicitly when space is itself physically discrete. Mesh differences measure observed stability. A finite-grid optimum certificate and a path sampling interval do not enclose continuum spatial error. When a verified upper bound overlaps the autonomous interval, record the unresolved or negative result at the stated certificate tolerance.

Run `python scripts/verify_organization.py` from the repository root to reproduce the six certified finite-grid comparisons and the independent 20,000-path run for each of three arms on the 32-node-per-axis grid. The receipt records the pre-run SHA-256 of its Python dependencies and aborts if any of those files change during execution. The path confidence band tests independent lattice dynamics against the production solver at 201 observation times. Its precision is much coarser than the separation being certified by the interval calculation.
