# Originality audit for the two-complex spatial repair pilot

17 November 2026

**Decision.** Proceed with a targeted numerical extension of Study 05. Volume reactivity, reversible spatial kernels, loss of information under spatial averaging, and finite-resource first-passage reliability all have precedents. The remaining question is quantitative. How large is the error in functional survival when the count chain has been calibrated independently on a uniform encounter, then used without refitting on different spatial preparations and through repeated fuel-consuming and fuel-regenerating transitions? The literature comparison and the reductions below furnish a reason to measure that error. They establish no original theorem or experimentally calibrated chemistry.

## Scope of the comparison

Two labelled complexes in a periodic cube of side \(L\) switch between healthy \(H\) and damaged \(D\). Their independent Brownian motions have constant diffusivities \(D_1,D_2\), so their relative position \(x\) is Brownian on \(\mathbb T_L^3\) with generator \(D_{\mathrm{rel}}\Delta\), where \(D_{\mathrm{rel}}=D_1+D_2\). A nonconstant Doi-type volume gate \(0\le g(x)\le1\) vanishes for \(|x|_{\rm per}\ge a\), with \(0<a<L/2\). The audit covers both the indicator gate of the initial specification and the smooth compact gate in the implementation. The fuel count \(b\) and waste count \(w=N-b\) enter propensities as well-mixed counts. Thermal damage and restoration have per-complex rates \(\eta\) and \(\eta/2\). An \(HD\to HH\) catalytic jump consumes one fuel at rate \(\kappa b g(x)\). Its labelled reverse at the destination has rate \(\kappa(w+1)g(x)/4\). The other labelled pair furnishes a second reverse. Positions persist across jumps. Both assembly directions are absent.

Take failure to be the first visit to \(DD\). For a fixed live chemical preparation \(s_0\), the observable \(S_\mu(t)=\Pr_{\mu,s_0}(\tau_{DD}>t)\) is a first-passage probability. A separately specified count chain uses the same stoichiometry, rate ratio and failure criterion. The proposed test compares \(\sup_{0\le t\le T}|S_\mu(t)-S_{\rm count}(t)|\) for uniform, explicit localized, and reaction-generated position laws. The plan's 0.01 absolute probability threshold is a screening convention. Numerical separation from the threshold requires an error assessment. ## Attempts to derive away the proposed advance

**Balance and fuel ledger.** The labelled equilibrium density is uniform in position with chemical weight proportional to \(2^{d}8^{-b}/[b!(N-b)!]\). The weight ratio across the catalytic edge is \(4b/(w+1)\). Both directions have the same \(g(x)\), so the common gate cancels wherever the edge is enabled, and both fluxes vanish off the gate. The ratio for one thermal \(H\to D\) is 2. Diffusion is reversible under uniform position. Combining labelled states multiplies their weight by \(\binom{2}{h}\), which gives the Study 05 marginal after normalization. With assembly absent, the identity \(J_R(t)=b(0)-b(t)\) follows path by path. This bounds net catalytic repairs by the initial fuel count. Reverse jumps regenerate fuel, so gross repairs need not obey that bound.

**A matched mean already allows timing differences before any resource feedback.** Freeze chemistry except for a forward repair clock at \(b_\star>0\), with \(\lambda=\kappa b_\star\). For the first such jump, \(A_t=\int_0^t g(X_u)\,du\) gives \(U_\mu(t)=\mathbb E_\mu[\exp(-\lambda A_t)]\). If \(\mu\) is uniform, write \(p=\int g\,d\mu\). Jensen gives \(U_\mu(t)\ge e^{-\lambda pt}\), and hence \(\mathbb E_\mu\tau\ge1/(\lambda p)\). The exponential rate \(k_\star=1/\mathbb E_\mu\tau\) is therefore at most \(\lambda p\). Its initial reaction hazard cannot generally reproduce the uniform hazard \(\lambda p\), let alone the centered contact start with initial hazard \(\lambda\) or a start strictly outside contact with initial hazard zero. With an independent competing clock of rate \(\gamma\), the measured probability is \(\mathbb E_\mu e^{-\gamma\tau}\). A mean calibration fixes only the derivative of this transform at \(\gamma=0\). This effect occurs with frozen fuel and a single forward jump. The pilot measures the size of the difference under repeated reversible chemistry.

**Spatial memory already appears in one projected jump.** With \(\mathcal F_{t-}^C\) the observed chemical history just before \(t\), the projected forward propensity before failure is \(\kappa b_{t-}\mathbf1_{\{h_{t-}=1\}}\mathbb E[g(X_t)\mid\mathcal F_{t-}^C]\). At a recorded catalytic event, \(g(X_t)>0\). An equilibrium event samples positions with density \(g/p\) with respect to uniform measure, so its expected gate value is \(\int g^2\,d\mu/p>p\) when \(g\) is nonconstant, where \(\mu\) is uniform. The conditional propensity makes the count-only projection history-dependent in general. There is an immediate failure of an exact count projection for all initial position laws, since point preparations on opposite sides of the gate have different initial chemical jump rates. A constant \(g\) gives the exact count process at any diffusion speed. A scalar time change by \(A_t\) solves the chemistry with thermal channels removed. When they run on laboratory time the thermal and catalytic matrices generally do not commute. Conditional on the Brownian path, the standard time-ordered evolution with generator \(Q_{\rm thermal}+g(X_t)Q_{\rm catalytic}\) describes the coupled process.

**Proximity has two possible survival effects in the stipulated model.** For initial laws giving the gate boundary zero mass, the first two short-time terms follow by applying the killed generator twice to the constant live function. Write \(\bar g_\mu=\int g\,d\mu\). Starting in \(HD\) gives \(S'_\mu(0)=-\eta\) and \(S''_\mu(0+)=\eta(3\eta/2+\kappa b\bar g_\mu)\). Starting in \(HH\) gives \(S'_\mu(0)=0\) and \(S''_\mu(0+)=-\eta(2\eta+\kappa w\bar g_\mu/2)\). While \(b>0\) enables a proximity benefit from an \(HD\) start, \(w>0\) enables the reverse channel to reduce early survival from an \(HH\) start. This rules out a general claim that proximity improves survival. These local derivatives do not determine the ordering at the reported horizon.

## What would establish the benchmark result

- Calibrate the count chain from an uncensored uniform isolated reaction-time experiment at a specified fuel level, with the competing thermal channel disabled for that calibration. For a mean calibration report \(\kappa_{\rm count}=1/(b_\star\mathbb E_\mu\tau)\), and use the same factor in both catalytic directions to preserve the equilibrium ratio. If several isolated rates are calibrated per edge, document the convention and preserve every paired ratio. Keep these rates fixed for the survival comparisons. An independent check against the fast-diffusion average exposes the difference between a calibrated rate and \(\kappa p\) at finite diffusion.
- Specify each position law and the complete chemical launch. Distinguish a position law sampled at a reaction event from an imposed launch law that borrows the equilibrium event-biased position marginal. A synthetic launch can pair this marginal with chemical counts that are unreachable by a forward repair in the closed inventory. Use the same chemical counts in the uniform comparison.
- Report the time window, error and refinement evidence, first-failure survival for every launch, and the signed as well as absolute discrepancy from the calibrated chain. Conditioning on a catalytic event can change both the chemical launch and the spatial law, so a comparison that changes both together cannot isolate the role of position.

This audit used targeted search and selected equations from the closest accessible primary texts. It did not exhaust diffusion theory, reliability, or chemical reaction networks, and a recent encounter-memory paper appeared only as a publisher excerpt because the full page returned HTTP 403. The search thus supports the numerical benchmark's scope. It does not certify the literature's completeness or a general novelty claim.
