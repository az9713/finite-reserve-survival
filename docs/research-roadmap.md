# Research gates after Study 05

Studies 02–04 added paid reversible repair, shared operating reserves, and autonomous reaction channels. Study 05 adds finite-inventory assembly of repair catalysts, a sharp count bound with an attaining path, a single-complex analytic optimum, and a certified interior two-complex optimum. See [the study](self-assembly.md) and [certificate replay](../results/assembly-certificate-check.json) for the exact scope.

The next unresolved gates are an explicit molecular/intermediate realization of the effective channels, independent proof review and specialist priority review, and optimization over a broader kinetic or feedback-controller family. Linking assembly to the earlier sensor/actuator model would require a new combined architecture with its own resource ledger.

## Original Study 01 roadmap, retained as historical context

1. **Priority check.** Extend the novelty search into Markov-modulated survival, dynamic disorder, and sensing reliability. Ask whether Proposition 2 or its combined use with the physical frontier is already explicit. Retain the result as a reproduction if so.
2. **Physical repair.** Replace free instantaneous damage reset by reversible transitions driven by a chemical affinity. Include maintenance and reset work in the same energy ledger. Test whether the finite-time ranking reversal survives.
3. **Finite resource reserve.** Couple work extraction and expenditures to a finite reservoir; rates change when resources are low. Distinguish average resource feasibility from first-passage failure. This is necessary before claiming self-maintenance.
4. **Action costs.** Add a physical actuator with finite delay and switching work, rather than identifying action with the sensor state. Derive when useful information is too expensive to act on.
5. **Adaptive capacity.** Permit the device to modulate K or A within physically specified switching costs. Solve the finite-horizon risk-sensitive control problem and compare against fixed designs.
6. **Implementation transfer.** Compare a two-state thermal memory, a chemical receptor network, and a mechanical sensor. Identify which dimensionless relationships remain invariant and which are artifacts of the model.

Success criterion: a justified generalization with a matching construction, or a decisive counterexample showing why the proposed generalization fails. Additional plots alone do not satisfy this criterion.
